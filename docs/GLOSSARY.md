# Plain-language glossary

## Accountable owner

The person or authorised body responsible for the outcome and for deciding whether to continue, change, stop or revisit the decision. An owner is not merely the person who enters data or manages a project plan.

## Capability

The real ability to produce an outcome. It may depend on people, knowledge, process, information, applications, technology, suppliers, contracts and authority together.

## Decision sufficiency

Having enough reliable and relevant evidence to make a defensible decision at the scale and reversibility of the change. It does not mean knowing everything.

## Disposition

The chosen treatment of a capability. Possible dispositions include preserve, improve, reuse, scale, integrate, consolidate, introduce a defined AI-enabled change, transform, retire or defer.

## Evidence provenance

The record of where information came from, who produced it, when it was current, what it covers and what its limitations are.

## Origin and recipient

The origin is the organisation, team or community where a capability currently works. The recipient is the organisation or team expected to adopt or benefit from it. A transfer is not successful unless the recipient can sustain it and has the authority to do so.

## Proportionate evidence

Evidence scaled to the decision. A reversible local trial needs less machinery than an irreversible, high-impact or cross-jurisdictional change. Proportionality never removes applicable rights or legal duties.

## Rationalization

A reasoned comparison of credible ways to meet a need while seeing the capability as a whole. It is not a synonym for cost cutting, centralisation, application removal or AI deployment.

## Rationalization.AI

The specific evidence-led technique contributed to the URF proposal. It frames one capability decision, applies the seven connected views proportionately, compares credible dispositions, records evidence and uncertainty, routes specialist questions, and establishes accountable review conditions.

## Review point

A stated time or condition at which the decision is reconsidered using updated evidence. It may lead to continuation, improvement, scaling, correction, withdrawal or retirement.

## Specialist handoff

The transfer of a defined question to the competent legal, safety, privacy, cybersecurity, labour, accessibility, financial, architectural or sector-specific process. URF records the question, owner and result; it does not replace specialist regimes.

## Seven connected views

Value and demand; people and knowledge; process and service; information and applications; technology and resilience; authority and obligations; and origin and recipient. They are perspectives on one decision, not separate audits or a score.

## Unified Rationalization Framework (URF)

A broader, platform-neutral structure for bringing a whole capability, its alternatives, evidence, authority, dependencies and review conditions into one accountable decision record. It can incorporate Rationalization.AI and other competent methods.

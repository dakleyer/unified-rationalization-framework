# Research lineage and evidence boundary

## Purpose

This note explains how the specific Rationalization.AI technique informed the broader Unified Rationalization Framework (URF), which ideas were retained, which claims were narrowed, and what still requires comparative evidence. It is a provenance record, not a claim that every earlier proposition has been validated.

## 1. Practical origin

The project began with enterprise architecture and rationalization practice in complex, decentralised organisations. The recurring problem was broader than application removal: a useful capability could depend on people, working knowledge, processes, information, systems, infrastructure, suppliers, contracts and local authority at the same time.

That practice led to three durable observations:

- a technology inventory can mistake valuable local variation for duplication;
- a new demand can sometimes be met by improving or transferring an existing capability; and
- an AI proposal should be compared with preservation, improvement, reuse, scaling, integration, consolidation, non-AI change and justified deferral.

Historical practice is a field anchor. It does not, by itself, validate later claims about AI readiness or universal effectiveness.

## 2. The 2025 research programme

Internal study material prepared in June 2025 framed **AI Ready-Up Rationalization** as a possible research programme. It proposed examining whether organisational clarity, strategic alignment and simplification before an AI investment could improve readiness and reduce avoidable cost.

The material proposed hypotheses, a cross-sector survey, interviews, case studies and a maturity framework. These were research intentions. They were not represented as completed survey results, a measured sample of 250 organisations, a validated index or an established causal relationship.

The lasting contribution of that programme is the research question: can a whole-capability decision method improve the quality, traceability and sustainability of AI and non-AI choices?

## 3. The 2026 consolidation

The 2026 work converted the broad research idea into a bounded decision method. The main corrections were:

- the unit of review became a complete capability rather than an application portfolio alone;
- AI became one possible disposition, not the presumed objective;
- preservation, improvement, reuse, scaling and no-change options became explicit;
- origin and recipient readiness were added for transfers between organisations;
- decision rights, affected people and specialist handoffs were made visible;
- evidence was made proportionate to the decision rather than exhaustive by default; and
- claims of novelty or superiority were replaced by testable hypotheses and counterexamples.

The method is intentionally compatible with enterprise architecture, portfolio management, process improvement, investment governance, NIST AI RMF, ISO guidance, OECD AI principles and applicable sector regimes. Compatibility does not mean conformity or endorsement.

## 4. Prior-method challenge

URF should not be treated as distinctive merely because it combines familiar disciplines. Its contribution must be tested against competent existing practice.

Four useful challenges are:

1. If TOGAF, ArchiMate, application-portfolio management or another established method already produces the same complete decision at the same effort and quality, URF may add only a communication template.
2. If existing IT-governance guidance already provides the same cross-organisation authority, alternative and recipient tests, URF's claims should be narrowed accordingly.
3. NIST AI RMF already recognises non-AI options. URF must therefore show value in understanding and transferring existing capabilities, not claim to have invented the non-AI alternative.
4. Publication in a catalogue or participation in a reference group is not proof of originality, adoption or effectiveness.

## 5. Current evidence classes

The project keeps three classes separate:

| Class | What it contains | What it can support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Historical practice | Documented observations from enterprise work | Plausibility and design insight |
| Method abstraction | The G0.11 framework, templates and diagrams | Expert review and controlled use |
| Prospective evaluation | Comparative cases and measures still to be conducted | Future findings about reproducibility, decision quality and operational value |

No finding in the third class is claimed until a study is completed and reported with its limitations.

These internal documents are listed for provenance only. Their private comments, correspondence, customer evidence, speculative numerical illustrations and unpublished third-party material are not reproduced in this repository.

## 7. Claims deliberately excluded

G0.11 does not claim:

- a completed multi-centre or 250-participant study;
- universal savings, exponential complexity reduction or a validated maturity score;
- that URF is the first rationalization, architecture or AI-governance method;
- that one historical programme proves general AI-readiness effects;
- conformity with law or standards merely because they are referenced; or
- OECD, GPAI, client, standards-body or reference-group adoption or endorsement.

Those boundaries are part of the method's integrity and should remain visible in every future release.

## Controlled detailed description for the form

Rationalization.AI is a specific practice and research technique proposed by Iván Abril Palma and stewarded through The Integral Management Society (IMSV), with public research and implementation context from Tegrity.AI and JubAp.eu. The broader Unified Rationalization Framework (URF) is a proposed platform-neutral structure through which Rationalization.AI and other competent methods can contribute to one accountable decision. The two names are related but are not interchangeable.

The technique addresses a recurring problem: an AI product or pilot may be selected before the organisation has established what capability already exists, who owns it, which people and knowledge sustain it, what information and dependencies it requires, who may authorise change and where value must be realised. This can duplicate discovery, hide lifecycle burden, erase valuable local variation and automate an unresolved organisational decision.

Rationalization.AI is applied to one bounded decision. It frames the outcome and baseline; describes the material parts of the capability through seven connected views—value and demand; people and knowledge; process and service; information and applications; technology and resilience; authority and obligations; and origin and recipient; compares credible dispositions; records evidence and uncertainty; routes specialist questions to the competent regime; and establishes accountable implementation, review and stopping conditions.

The credible dispositions include preserve, improve, reuse or replicate, scale or federate, integrate or consolidate, introduce a defined AI-enabled change, make another non-AI change, transform, merge or retire, and defer with a reason, owner and review point. The views are not seven separate audits or a universal score. Evidence is proportionate to the impact and reversibility of the decision.

The public package includes the G0.11 framework, a scientific annex, a decision-record template, a comparative evaluation protocol, a research-lineage and evidence-boundary note, a glossary, diagrams and a controlled submission-attribution report.

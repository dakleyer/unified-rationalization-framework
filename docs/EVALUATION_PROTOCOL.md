# Evaluation protocol for controlled field studies

## 1. Purpose

This protocol is for organisations that want to test the Rationalization.AI technique within the broader Unified Rationalization Framework (URF) against a competent existing decision method. It is a prospective research design, not evidence that either the technique or the framework has already been validated.

The central question is:

> Does a whole-capability, alternatives-first decision record improve decision quality, traceability and sustained value compared with the organisation's competent existing method?

## 2. Suitable cases

A case should involve a real choice with at least two credible alternatives and an identifiable decision owner. Examples include:

- whether to preserve, improve, transfer, consolidate or replace an existing capability;
- whether a proposed AI use is preferable to a non-AI change;
- whether a proven capability in one organisation can be reused or scaled elsewhere;
- whether a pilot should continue, change, stop or return to discovery; or
- how to decide across several business units, suppliers or jurisdictions.

Do not select only cases likely to make URF look favourable. Include difficult cases, decisions where no change is reasonable and cases where an existing method may already be sufficient.

## 3. Comparator

The comparator should be a competent version of the organisation's current practice, not an artificial weak baseline. It may be an established investment paper, enterprise-architecture method, application-portfolio process, NIST AI RMF workflow, procurement route or sector-specific governance process.

Before the comparison begins, record:

- what the comparator normally produces;
- the people and evidence it uses;
- its expected effort and decision time;
- the obligations it already satisfies; and
- where URF is expected to add value, duplicate work or create burden.

Where a parallel comparison is impractical, use a structured retrospective comparison and state the limitations.

## 4. Minimum design

1. **Freeze the question.** Define the need, affected capability, decision owner, scope, exclusions and decision date.
2. **Record the baseline.** Capture current performance, cost, delay, risk, quality or service measures relevant to the outcome.
3. **List alternatives before selecting one.** Include preservation, improvement, reuse or scaling, integration, consolidation, defined AI use, non-AI change, retirement and justified deferral where credible.
4. **Apply the comparator and URF.** Keep time, participants and evidence access reasonably comparable.
5. **Use independent review.** Ask reviewers who did not design URF to assess completeness, clarity and decision defensibility.
6. **Follow the decision.** Record what was authorised, what was implemented and which assumptions changed.
7. **Requalify at the review point.** Decide whether to continue, change, scale, stop or return to discovery.

## 5. Core measures

Measures should be selected before the case is analysed.

### Decision coverage

- number and relevance of credible alternatives considered;
- whether preservation, reuse, scaling and non-AI options remained visible;
- accurate identification of valued local exceptions;
- visibility of origin, recipient and transfer conditions; and
- completeness of authority, affected-party and specialist handoffs.

### Evidence quality

- proportion of material assertions with an identifiable source and date;
- important unknowns made explicit rather than filled by assumption;
- duplicated discovery avoided through reuse of existing evidence; and
- conflicts or stale evidence identified before approval.

### Decision quality

- clarity of the outcome, baseline and accountable owner;
- visibility of dependencies, full lifecycle burden and implementation capacity;
- reviewers' ability to explain why the chosen option was preferred;
- number of material issues discovered only after approval; and
- ability to revise the decision when assumptions change.

### Operational value

- decision lead time and analyst effort;
- avoidable intervention, migration or procurement cost;
- benefit realisation against the stated baseline;
- continuity, security, quality and user effects; and
- successful or failed transfer of the capability to a recipient.

Do not combine these measures into a universal score unless the weighting and validity of that score have been independently established.

## 6. Seven-view evidence prompts

| View | Minimum evaluation prompt |
| --- | --- |
| Value and demand | Was the outcome, beneficiary and baseline clear, and was existing value protected? |
| People and knowledge | Were skills, tacit knowledge, capacity and human effects visible? |
| Process and service | Were real work, exceptions, handovers and continuity understood? |
| Information and applications | Were meanings, provenance, rights, systems and interfaces sufficiently clear? |
| Technology and resilience | Were infrastructure, security, lifecycle cost, suppliers, rollback and continuity considered? |
| Authority and obligations | Were decision rights, affected people and applicable duties identified and routed correctly? |
| Origin and recipient | If value moved, was the origin respected and was the recipient able and authorised to sustain it? |

## 7. Counterexamples and failure conditions

A credible evaluation actively looks for evidence against the method. Report when:

- the comparator produces an equally complete decision with less effort;
- the seven views add no material information;
- URF delays a reversible, low-impact decision without improving it;
- a transfer fails because recipient readiness was misjudged;
- local value is still lost despite the method;
- decision authority remains ambiguous;
- evidence collection becomes a second, duplicate inventory;
- the method is used as a score or as an automatic approval engine; or
- a specialist regime answers the question better and URF adds only administrative burden.

These results are findings, not reasons to hide a case.

## 8. People, rights and data protection

- Obtain the permissions required for organisational, personal and customer information.
- Minimise personal data and separate research consent from operational authority.
- Do not publish confidential customer evidence, private correspondence or protected know-how.
- Credit contributors on an opt-in basis and distinguish authorship, review, participation and institutional endorsement.
- Record conflicts of interest and any role played by IMSV, Tegrity.AI, JubAp.eu or implementation suppliers.
- Keep specialist legal, labour, accessibility, safety, cybersecurity and sector decisions with the competent authorities.

## 9. Reporting format

Each case report should state:

1. question, scope and exclusions;
2. comparator and why it was competent;
3. participants, authority and conflicts;
4. baseline and selected measures;
5. alternatives and material evidence;
6. decision and implementation conditions;
7. results at the review point;
8. counterevidence, failed assumptions and unintended effects;
9. transferability limits; and
10. data, documents or methods that cannot be disclosed.

Positive and negative findings should be reported together. Catalogue listing, participation in a reference group or publication of a case does not convert the result into institutional endorsement.

## 10. Release gate

Before describing an evaluation as completed, confirm that:

- the case was real rather than hypothetical;
- the comparator and measures were stated in advance or the retrospective limitation is explicit;
- reviewers and decision owners are identified by role;
- results are tied to the original baseline and review point;
- negative findings are included;
- permissions and attribution are documented; and
- the public statement does not exceed the evidence.
